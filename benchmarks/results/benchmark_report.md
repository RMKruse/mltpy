## Methodology

- For each `(n, order, censoring)` cell the same Python-generated input dataset is fitted by both backends.
- Each cell is timed across multiple repetitions; medians and IQRs are computed over the converged reps only.
- pymlt: `pymlt.MLT(order, support).fit(...)`; R: `mlt::mlt(ctm(Bernstein_basis(...)), data=...)`.
- Right-censored cells use `CensoredData.right_censored` (Python) and `Surv(y, status)` (R), with ~30% censoring drawn from a deterministic seed.
- `Speedup` is `R median / Python median`; values > 1 mean pymlt is faster.

## Interpretation

Across 24 comparable cells, pymlt is on geometric mean **2.52× the speed of R mlt**, and is the faster backend in 24 of 24 cells. By censoring scheme: NONE 2.38×, RIGHT 2.68×. The largest speedup is at n=5000, order=4, censoring=RIGHT (4.59×); the smallest is at n=100, order=4, censoring=NONE (1.45×). Both backends now use the same PHR augmented Lagrangian solver — pymlt's pure-Python ``_auglag.py`` and R `mlt`'s `alabama::auglag` share the outer multiplier-update loop and the inner BFGS sub-problem, so iteration counts at the same precision land in the same ballpark and the divergent-basin caveat that applied under SLSQP no longer holds.  pymlt is now the faster backend in every cell, and the size of the margin is set by `n`.  At small `n` the per-fit cost is dominated by the fixed per-evaluation overhead inside each outer step — the distribution calls and design-matrix assembly that recur on every likelihood/gradient evaluation.  Replacing the `scipy.stats` frozen-distribution objects with direct `scipy.special` ufuncs (`log_ndtr`, `ndtr`, `expit`, `log_expit`, …) and memoising the assembled Bernstein `(B, dB)` matrices removed the Python/SciPy call overhead that previously let R lead the n=100–1000 cells, so pymlt now edges ahead even there.  At large `n` the per-fit cost shifts to vectorised linear algebra over the Bernstein design matrix — which pymlt evaluates once per fit and caches (it depends only on `y` and the basis order, not on the coefficients) — and the margin widens to several-fold.  The augmented Lagrangian also freezes its penalty ρ once the constraints are feasible and accepts the alabama-style feasible-and-stalled convergence point, so fits no longer burn the full outer-iteration budget on degenerate active sets.  Absolute per-fit times of a few milliseconds at small `n` mean timing noise can shift individual cells by 10–30%, so treat small-`n` ratios as indicative rather than precise.
